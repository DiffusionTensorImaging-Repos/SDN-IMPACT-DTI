Background reading · situating the findings

The literature your study sits in

~22 papers across motivated memory, the VTA→hippocampus circuit, white-matter microstructure, memory fidelity vs. error, lateralization, and positivity bias. Each is a citation + a distilled paragraph that keeps the method in view. ~10-minute read.

The one to know cold: Elliott, Murty, Brewer & McClure (2022) — SN/VTA↔hippocampus structural connectivity selectively predicts motivated memory. Your study extends that from tract-level connectivity to along-tract microstructure (FA + NODDI) of the reconstructed tract, in mothers, and adds positivity bias.

1 · Motivated memory: reward shapes what sticks

Adcock, Thangavel, Whitfield-Gabrieli, Knutson & Gabrieli (2006), Neuron

fMRI monetary-incentive encoding: a cue announced how much money was at stake for remembering the upcoming scene, tested 24 h later. High-value cues that preceded later-remembered (not forgotten) scenes drove the VTA, nucleus accumbens, and hippocampus, and the trial-by-trial VTA–hippocampus co-activation before the scene even appeared predicted whether it stuck. Located reward's memory benefit in anticipatory mesolimbic engagement — dopamine priming the hippocampus before encoding.

Wittmann, Schott, Guderian, Frey, Heinze & Düzel (2005), Neuron

Participants learned which pictures predicted monetary reward, then were scanned viewing them. Reward-predicting pictures activated the SN/VTA and hippocampus, and the strength of that midbrain activation was associated with better recognition memory three weeks later. Tied dopaminergic-midbrain engagement to durable, hippocampus-dependent consolidation, not just immediate encoding.

Murty & Adcock (2014), Cerebral Cortex

fMRI: high-reward motivational states reorganized large-scale cortical networks so the hippocampus became better at detecting unexpected events ("enriched encoding"). Motivation doesn't just raise memory globally — it tunes hippocampal sensitivity to what's worth encoding. (Murty is a co-author, and in your meeting.)

Gruber, Ritchey, Wang, Doss & Ranganath (2016), Neuron

Reward-memory fMRI where the key window was the post-encoding rest period: hippocampal activity and hippocampal–VTA coupling after learning predicted preferential later memory for high-reward items. Extends the reward-memory story into offline consolidation.

2 · The VTA→hippocampus circuit (and who supplies the dopamine)

Lisman & Grace (2005), Neuron

The theoretical backbone — the "hippocampal–VTA loop." The hippocampus detects novelty, relays it via subiculum→accumbens→ventral pallidum to the VTA, which fires and releases dopamine back into the hippocampus, enhancing LTP and gating what enters long-term memory. The reason a VTA→hippocampus pathway should matter at all.

Kahn & Shohamy (2013), Hippocampus

Resting-state fMRI: the SN/VTA, nucleus accumbens, and hippocampus show coordinated intrinsic functional connectivity in humans, and individual differences in that coupling relate to memory. Evidence the anatomical loop runs as a functional unit even at rest, not only during reward tasks.

Kempadoo, Mosharov, Choi, Sulzer & Kandel (2016), PNAS · with Takeuchi et al. (2016), Nature

The important caveat. Two optogenetic rodent studies overturned the assumption that the VTA supplies the hippocampus's dopamine: much of the dopamine driving dorsal-hippocampal spatial learning and novelty-linked memory persistence comes from locus coeruleus neurons that co-release dopamine and noradrenaline — VTA dopamine axons are surprisingly sparse in dorsal hippocampus. This is exactly why you should frame your tract anatomically (VTA→hippocampus white matter), not as "the dopamine pathway": the transmitter source is genuinely contested, and diffusion MRI can't see neurotransmitter identity.

3 · Structural connectivity — the direct predecessor

Elliott, Murty, Brewer & McClure (2022), Journal of Neuroscience

The closest prior study. Using diffusion-MRI probabilistic tractography, individual differences in structural connectivity between the SN/VTA and hippocampus selectively predicted the reward-related memory benefit (motivated memory) — and did not predict reward-insensitive memory. A structural correlate specific to motivated encoding. Your work pushes this from "how connected" to "what the tissue along the tract looks like" (FA + NODDI), in mothers, with positivity bias added.

Elliott, Blais, McClure & Brewer (2019), Neurobiology of Learning & Memory

EEG + behavior: the boost reward value gives recognition memory was carried by a dopaminergic reward-valuation signal (the P3 event-related potential), not by extra rehearsal. Places the value→memory effect at reward evaluation during encoding.

Elliott, McClure & Brewer (2020), Cognition

Behavioral individual-difference work: people differ reliably and stably in how much they prioritize high-value items in memory (value-directed remembering). Motivated-memory ability is a trait — which is precisely what a microstructure predictor would explain.

4 · White-matter microstructure predicts memory

Metzler-Baddeley, Jones, Belaroussi, Aggleton & O'Sullivan (2011), Journal of Neuroscience

Diffusion-MRI tractography across aging: degraded fornix microstructure (the hippocampus's main output tract) specifically predicted poorer episodic recall, beyond other frontotemporal tracts and gray-matter volume. Establishes that hippocampal-pathway white-matter microstructure — not just the gray matter — tracks memory.

Hodgetts, Postans, Warne, Bendel, Aggleton, Postans... Graham (2020), Cortex

In healthy young adults, FA of the pre-commissural fornix (hippocampus→prefrontal), but not the post-commissural branch, correlated with the episodic richness of both remembered past and imagined future events. Sub-tract microstructural differences map onto specific memory functions — the rationale for along-tract, branch-specific analysis like yours.

5 · NODDI — reading tissue beyond FA

Nazeri, Chakravarty, Rotenberg, Rajji, Rathi, Michael & Voineskos (2015), Journal of Neuroscience

Applied NODDI across the adult lifespan and showed that neurite density (NDI) — including in gray matter — declines with age and relates to cognitive performance, sometimes where FA is silent. Established NDI/ODI as functionally meaningful microstructure metrics beyond the diffusion tensor.

Kohli et al. (2021), Alzheimer's & Dementia

Direct biological validation: hippocampal NODDI neurite density (NDI) correlated with synaptic density measured by [11C]UCB-J PET in the same people. So hippocampal NDI is a real proxy for neurite/synaptic density — the tissue meaning behind your left-HPC-NDI finding.

Temporal-lobe cortical NODDI & memory (2024–25), human MRI

Recent human studies report medial-temporal / temporal-cortical NODDI indices (NDI, ODI) tracking memory performance, and being more sensitive than tensor FA to memory-relevant tissue change (including early Alzheimer's). Supports characterizing a memory pathway with NODDI, not FA alone.

6 · Memory fidelity vs. error (what d′ and false memories index)

Yassa & Stark (2011), Trends in Neurosciences

Review of hippocampal pattern separation — the dentate-gyrus/CA3 circuit that orthogonalizes similar experiences into distinct traces. When it fails (e.g., aging), similar events blur and false recognitions rise. The mechanistic account of memory fidelity vs. interference — the accuracy your d′ captures.

Bakker, Kirwan, Miller & Stark (2008), Science

High-resolution fMRI during a mnemonic-similarity task: CA3/dentate treated tiny changes to a studied item as new (separation), while CA1 generalized. First direct human evidence for subfield-specific separation — grounds "false vs. accurate memory" in an identifiable hippocampal computation.

Schacter, Guerin & St. Jacques (2011), Trends in Cognitive Sciences

Review: memory errors (false recognitions, gist distortions) are a byproduct of an adaptive, reconstructive system, with the hippocampus/MTL supporting true and false recollection alike. Frames false memories — your FABias — as informative signal, not just noise.

7 · Lateralization of hippocampal memory

Golby et al. (2001), Brain · and the material-specific model (Learn. Mem. 2002)

Across fMRI and epilepsy-lesion work, encoding lateralizes by material: verbal / nameable content engages the left MTL-hippocampus, spatial / pictorial content the right. The standard reason to expect — and to interpret — left-vs-right hippocampal effects. Your social faces are nameable, socially-labeled material, which plausibly weights left, consistent with your left-lateralized NDI effect.

8 · Positivity bias — what we choose to remember

Mather & Carstensen (2005), Trends in Cognitive Sciences

Review of the "positivity effect": a motivated bias toward remembering positive over negative information, framed by socioemotional selectivity theory as goal-driven emotion regulation and implemented by prefrontal control over amygdala/hippocampal encoding. The conceptual home for a positivity bias in memory — your HitRateBias / FABias. (Related: negative material tends to be remembered with more detail/vividness, positive more gist-like.)

"Untangling the threads of motivated memory" (2024), Psychonomic Bulletin & Review

Recent work dissociates reward-based and emotion-based influences on memory as independent mechanisms rather than one unified system. Directly relevant: your task pits monetary reward against social/emotional material, and they may not share a substrate — which fits your social-specific, monetary-null pattern.
